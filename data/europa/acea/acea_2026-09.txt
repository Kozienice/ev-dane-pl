New car registrations: +5.7% in September 2026
year-to-date; battery-electric 22.7% market share
The new EU passenger car market showed notable resilience, rising 5.7% year-to-date
despite a challenging macroeconomic environment marked by persistently rising energy
prices and geopolitical headwinds. Demand for a range of electrified vehicles remained
strong, particularly in major European markets, supported by buyer support measures and a
broader model offering. Hybrid-electric vehicles ranked as the most popular powertrain
choice among buyers, whereas battery-electric cars and plug-in hybrids accounted for 22.7%
and 10.1% of the EU market, respectively.
NEW EU CAR REGISTRATIONS BY POWER SOURCE
Up until September 2026, battery-electric cars secured a 22.7% share of the EU market, up
from 16.1% a year earlier. Hybrid-electric car registrations captured 36.3% of the market,
remaining the preferred choice among EU consumers. At the same time, the combined
market share of petrol and diesel cars fell to 28.2%, compared with 36.9% a year earlier.
Data source: the European Automobile Manufacturers’ Association (ACEA), based on aggregated data provided
by national automobile associations, ACEA members, and Mobility Global.
The current year data contain provisional figures and may therefore be subject to future revision.

Electric cars
In the first nine months of 2026, 1,931,465 new battery-electric cars were registered,
capturing 22.7% of the EU market and establishing them as the second most popular
powertrain choice year-to-date. Three of the four largest EU markets, which together
accounted for 64% of all battery-electric car registrations, recorded strong growth: France
(+79.1%), Germany (+58.1%), and Denmark (+40.3%). Belgium also posted a more
moderate 15.7% increase.
September 2026 YTD’s figures also showed new EU hybrid-electric car registrations rising
to 3,090,530 units, supported by growth in Italy (+20.8%) and Spain (+20.5%), alongside
more modest gains in Germany (+4.9%) and France (+0.6%). Overall, hybrid-electric models
accounted for 36.3% of the total EU market.
Plug-in-hybrid electric car registrations continued to grow, reaching 860,257 units in
January-September 2026. This underlying trend was driven by rising volumes in key
markets such as Italy (+72.4%), Spain (+34.7%), and Germany (+13.7%). New plug-in-hybrid
electric cars represent 10.1% of EU registrations, up from 9% over the same period in 2025.
Petrol and diesel cars
By the end of September 2026, petrol car registrations continued to decline, falling by
19.1%, with significant downturns across all major European markets. France saw the
sharpest decline, with volumes plunging by 36.1%, while other key markets also posted
double-digit losses: Germany (-22.7%), Spain (-19%), and Italy (-16.4%).
With 1,802,078 new cars registered over the past nine months, petrol’s market share
dropped to 21.2% from 27.7% last year. Additionally, the diesel car market continued its
downward trend, with registrations declining by 19.5% and representing 7.1% of new EU car
registrations, down from 9.3%.
